## Why we are asking

Our coach flagged the same gap twice: we designed for Meena without having
talked to a Meena. We have the volume evidence — Coimbatore's draft Master Plan
2041 drew over 3,000 objections on a 600 page document and the Local Planning
Authority went through them individually over roughly two months; Pimpri
Chinchwad received 49,000 on one revised development plan; the DDA processed
over 33,000. What we do not have is which part of that work is actually slow,
and what gets lost when it is rushed.

Those two answers are the point of this call. Everything else is optional.

## The ask, in one message

> I'm a student building a tool for the Tech for Good hackathon in Coimbatore.
> It reads the written objections filed on a draft plan and groups them by the
> argument being made, so the themes and who raised them are visible without
> reading all of them end to end.
>
> I don't want to pitch it — I want to check whether the problem I've assumed
> is the real one. Could I take 20 minutes to ask how consultation responses
> are actually processed today, and where the time really goes? Happy to work
> around your schedule.

Twenty minutes, not forty five. It is easier to say yes to, and it usually
runs long once they start talking.

## Questions

Ask the first two properly. The rest only if there is time.

**1. Walk me through the last consultation you handled, start to finish.**
Let them talk. Do not steer. Note where they pause or sigh — that is the
expensive step, and it is often not the one we assumed.

Listen specifically for: is the slow part *reading* the responses, or
*deduplicating* them, or *attributing* them to organisations, or *writing* the
summary for the committee? We built for reading. If the real cost is the write
up or the attribution, that changes what we should show first.

**2. When there are too many to read carefully, what actually happens?**
This is the "missing real themes" question, and it is the one to get concrete
about. Push gently for a specific instance:

- Does anything get skipped outright, or does everything get skimmed?
- Has a concern ever surfaced at the hearing that wasn't in the summary?
- Who notices, and what does it cost when it happens?

**3. How do you handle one organisation filing a very long submission?**
Our ranking counts distinct organisations, not text chunks, because a twelve
page submission arriving as twelve rows made one body look like twelve. Check
whether that reflects how they actually weigh submissions — they may weight a
detailed technical objection *more*, not less, in which case our default view
is wrong for them.

**4. What does the output need to be?**
A ranked list on a screen, or a document that goes into a committee pack? If
it has to survive a committee, what has to be in it — quotes, counts,
attribution, page references?

**5. Would a summary a machine produced be usable at all?**
Ask plainly. If it can't be cited or checked, it may be unusable regardless of
accuracy, and that tells us the quote-per-theme feature matters more than the
ranking does.

**6. If this existed, how would you actually get it?**
Coach question. A link someone sends, something IT has to approve, a file you
run? Shapes whether a hosted URL is enough.
